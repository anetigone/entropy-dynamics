\section{Interpretation and Remaining Gaps}
The evidence supports a layered explanation. Mixed shifts determine displacement from the entropy peak and can reverse direction. Nonlinear geometry and risk endpoints determine transit times. Independent calibration must resolve geometry on the shrinking SGD scale. In slow far-field passage, continued noise can materially broaden time distributions beyond a near-peak seed description. These mechanisms explain different failures and should not be collapsed into one correction.

\paragraph{A prediction theory, with conditional visual evidence.}
The declared scalar exit statements retain actual entropy updates, risk comparators, and discrete time. Correct target empirical geometry explains many visual cells, but the oracle result is not independent prediction. Larger independent field samples improve forecasts without solving label-dependent endpoints. The long-tail diagnosis is supported by interventions in three single-side-dominated settings; its extension to multiple branches is open. Neither this diagnosis nor fixed-source scans contradict a critical asymptotic theorem with geometry-dependent constants.

\paragraph{Finite pool versus population.}
Each visual pair has 1000 official test images per class. The fixed-pool empirical risk is exactly computable, yet a conservative uniform uncertainty bound for its difference from frozen risk is about .09362, larger than $\epsilon=.02$. This does not assert that the error is actually that large. It prevents presenting a precise finite-pool crossing time as a certified population crossing time. Millions of repeated calibration observations do not enlarge the image pool.

\paragraph{What remains to establish.}
The most direct next test is an independently specified combination of early-seed uncertainty and accumulated passage noise, evaluated on mixed-direction settings and another class pair. A separate target-centered calibration experiment is needed to test the random forecast limit. Mathematical work remains to independently verify the supplied proofs and align full-domain assumptions, initialization, boundary conventions, and component jumps with each empirical case. A practical unlabeled stop additionally requires identifiable risk-boundary information. Scale/LA comparisons and full BN experiments remain absent; no benchmark superiority is claimed.

\paragraph{Novelty boundary.}
Instability, logarithmic escape, and imbalance-related degradation are known. The contribution must rest on the risk-specific laws, tolerance distinction, real-SGD connection, and calibration consequences rather than another demonstration of collapse. These experiments support that narrower research direction; they do not settle the complete novelty comparison.

\section{Conclusion}
Risk exit under entropy adaptation depends jointly on distributional geometry, tolerance, stochastic scale, and calibration. A scalar analysis yields explicit direction and time laws. Controlled visual evidence shows substantial explanatory power when empirical geometry is correct, and exposes distinct calibration, boundary, and continuing-noise limitations. Establishing independent unlabeled risk prediction requires resolving those remaining information and approximation gaps.
