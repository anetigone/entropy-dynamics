\section{Gaussian certificates and real-SGD confirmation}\label{gaussian-certificates-and-real-sgd-confirmation}

\subsection{Gaussian decision certificates}\label{gaussian-decision-certificates}

A finite prior supplies a Bayes lower bound, and its finite-decimal posterior-median step rule supplies an upper bound. The certificate encloses the true posterior crossings and normal probabilities with directed arithmetic. An interpolation remainder based on a risk-curvature bound controls the entire continuous parameter window between all loss kinks. It neither assumes the prior is exactly least favorable nor relies on optimizer convergence.

\subsection{Two uncertainty layers}\label{two-uncertainty-layers}

Allocate .025 error to reference paths and .025 to calibration losses. At each cap, right-exit probability by the cap is below eventual \(\pi_n\), while the probability of not having exited left is above it. One-sided exact binomial bounds bracket these probabilities with a union bound over cells; possible population censoring is thus covered even when no sampled paths are censored.

Conditional on the independent reference sample, losses lie in {[}0,1{]} and paired differences in {[}-1,1{]}. Two-sided empirical Bernstein bounds \cite{maurer2009}, with a union bound over 40 rule risks and 30 paired gains, control calibration error. Add the reference error to each rule-risk bound and twice that error to paired differences. Propagate cellwise risk intervals through maxima and differences. This provides simultaneous coverage at least 95\% under the declared iid experiment, including both uncertainty layers. The beta quantiles use floating-point routines, so these statistical intervals are distinct from the directed Gaussian numerical certificate.

Complete design, uncertainty formulas, cellwise tables and original raw arrays are in the confirmation report. Results and code stay in their original directories. The earlier pilot is exploratory and is not pooled into these intervals.

\subsection{Constructing a certified minimax bracket}\label{constructing-a-certified-minimax-bracket}

Choose any finite prior with ordered support t\_1\textless\ldots\textless t\_m in {[}-K,K{]} and positive weights w\_i. Its posterior median under absolute loss is a Bayes rule taking the values Phi(t\_i). The boundary between actions k and k+1 solves

\[
\sum_{i\le k}w_i e^{x t_i/r^2-t_i^2/(2r^2)}
=\sum_{i>k}w_i e^{x t_i/r^2-t_i^2/(2r^2)}.
\tag{G.8}
\]

The ratio of the left sum to the right sum is strictly decreasing: its log derivative is the difference of two tilted means divided by \(r^2\), and every left support point is smaller than every right support point. Its limits are infinity and zero. Thus every crossing is unique; the ordered crossings define the posterior-median rule.

For any step rule with thresholds b\_j and action values a\_j, its exact risk is

\[
R_d(t)=\sum_j |a_j-\Phi(t)|
\left[\Phi\left(\frac{b_{j+1}-t}{r}\right)
      -\Phi\left(\frac{b_j-t}{r}\right)\right],
\tag{G.9}
\]

including b\_0=-infinity and the last boundary +infinity. Therefore the full unbounded observation domain is integrated through CDF differences, with no omitted integration tail.

The code optimizes a finite prior using floating-point quadrature only to find a candidate. It then rounds support points, prior numerators and rule thresholds to explicit finite decimals. These rounded quantities define the actual certified prior and rule.

For each true crossing, directed interval arithmetic verifies opposite signs of (G.8) at endpoints l\_j and u\_j, with the candidate threshold inside the bracket. The candidate rule and exact Bayes rule can differ only on the union of these strips. Gaussian mixture density is at most 1/(r sqrt(2 pi)); loss differences are at most one. Hence

\[
\text{Bayes risk}\ge
\sum_i w_i R_d(t_i)-\frac{\sum_j(u_j-l_j)}{r\sqrt{2\pi}}.
\tag{G.10}
\]

A lower enclosure for the right side is a valid minimax lower bound. This does not assume that the candidate prior is least favorable, that the search objective was computed exactly, or that the optimizer converged.

\subsection{Continuous-window upper bound}\label{continuous-window-upper-bound}

The candidate step rule itself gives an upper bound once sup\_\{\textbar t\textbar\textless=K\} R\_d(t) is bounded. Its action values are exactly Phi(t\_i), so the only absolute-loss kinks are at the known support points t\_i. Between successive kinks, differentiation under the Gaussian integral gives

\[
|R_d''(t)|\le C_2:=1+2/r+2/r^2.
\tag{G.11}
\]

Indeed the target's first and second derivatives are bounded in absolute value by one; the sum of absolute bin-probability derivatives is bounded by E\textbar Z\textbar/r\textless=1/r, and that of second derivatives by E\textbar Z\textsuperscript{2-1\textbar/(r}2)\textless=2/\(r^2\). All action losses are bounded by one. The bound is deliberately conservative.

For a partition interval {[}a,b{]} with no kink in its interior, linear interpolation and (G.11) imply

\[
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}R_d(t)
\le\max\{R_d(a),R_d(b)\}+C_2(b-a)^2/8.
\tag{G.12}
\]

The partition includes every support point and both endpoints. Adaptive refinement produces an upper bound over the entire continuous window, not just the evaluated grid.

\subsection{Arithmetic and probability enclosures}\label{arithmetic-and-probability-enclosures}

\texttt{certify.py} uses Python Decimal with 90-digit precision and outward rounding for interval operations. Correctly rounded Decimal exponentials and square roots are expanded by a representable value on each side. Pi is enclosed by Machin's identity and alternating arctangent remainders. Normal CDF values are enclosed by integrating the Taylor expansion of exp(-x\^{}2/2), including a rigorous next-term remainder once the terms decrease. For \textbar x\textbar\textgreater=12 a Mills-ratio tail bound is used instead. Both negative and positive tails are retained in their intervals.

The optimizer's normal CDF, integration grid, stopping tolerance and floating-point objective do not enter the validity of the final bounds. The certificate relies on the stated mathematical bounds, the correctness of the supplied implementation, and the documented Decimal arithmetic; it is not a proof-assistant verification of the runtime.

The search gap and the certified gap are different quantities. The final upper-minus-lower interval accounts for an imperfect prior/rule pair as well as root, probability-evaluation and continuous-window errors.

\subsection{Using the fixed rule without the local parameter}\label{using-the-fixed-rule-without-the-local-parameter}

For an arbitrary measurable Gaussian rule, the direct risk-transfer construction applies it to c \(T_n\)/\(\sqrt\rho\). This uses the known baseline CDF \(F_M\), not the unknown environment parameter.

For each of the fixed finite step rules produced here, a simpler implementation is also asymptotically justified: apply it to Xhat=c \(\sqrt n\) hat\_theta, the raw empirical moment coordinate. Under the baseline, Xhat-c \(T_n\)/\(\sqrt\rho\) tends to zero in probability. The likelihood-ratio moment bounds used in (G.5) transfer this to the compact local family uniformly. The Gaussian statistic has a continuous density uniformly bounded on the local window, so the probability of lying near one of the finitely many thresholds tends to zero with the threshold neighborhood. Therefore the two implementations have uniformly vanishing risk difference.

This argument is for a fixed finite rule. Refining the rule with n so that the number of thresholds diverges requires additional control. No finite-n risk certificate for the SGD experiment follows merely from the Gaussian numerical certificate.

\subsection{Simultaneous reference and calibration uncertainty}\label{simultaneous-reference-and-calibration-uncertainty}

At the fixed cap, let \(p_R\) be the probability of a right exit by the cap and \(p_{NL}\) the probability of not having exited left by the cap. The eventual right-exit probability satisfies

\[
p_R\le\pi_n\le p_{NL}.
\]

For each cell use a one-sided exact Clopper--Pearson lower bound on \(p_R\) and upper bound on \(p_{NL}\), each with error \(\alpha_{\rm ref}\)/(2C). Their endpoints {[}l\_i,u\_i{]} jointly cover every cell's eventual \(\pi_n\) with probability at least 1-\(\alpha_{\rm ref}\). This remains valid if a cell has censored paths; lack of an observed censor is not treated as proof that its population censoring probability is zero.

Set \(\widehat p_i\)=\(N_R/N\) and \(e_i\)=max(\(\widehat p_i\)-l\_i,u\_i-\(\widehat p_i\)). On the reference event, \textbar{}\(\widehat p_i\)-pi\_i\textbar\textless=\(e_i\).

\subsubsection{Calibration loss uncertainty conditional on the reference sample}\label{calibration-loss-uncertainty-conditional-on-the-reference-sample}

For each rule and cell, score calibration repetition j by L\_j=\textbar forecast\_j-\(\widehat p_i\)\textbar. Conditional on the independent reference sample, these are iid in {[}0,1{]}. Paired baseline-minus-optimized loss differences are iid in {[}-1,1{]}.

For m observations taking values in an interval of width B, with unbiased sample variance \(S_m^2\), use the two-sided empirical Bernstein radius

\[
b_m(B,\delta)=\sqrt{\frac{2S_m^2\log(4/\delta)}m}
+\frac{7B\log(4/\delta)}{3(m-1)}.
\]

This follows by scaling and applying both tails of Maurer--Pontil (2009), Theorem 4. Allocate \(\delta\)=\(\alpha_{\rm cal}\)/(7C) to each of 40 rule-risk estimates and 30 paired gains. Their conditional coverage is at least 1-\(\alpha_{\rm cal}\) by a union bound; integration over the reference sample preserves that bound. Independence between the different rules is not required.

For each rule risk, add \(e_i\) to its calibration radius. For a paired gain add 2\(e_i\), by the reverse triangle inequality. For the deterministic constant rule, propagate {[}l\_i,u\_i{]} through \textbar.5-p\textbar{} exactly instead of using an unnecessary calibration bound.

The resulting risk and gain intervals have \textbf{simultaneous coverage at least 95\%} over all reported cells and rules, under the declared iid simulation experiment. They include both uncertainty layers. They are not just conditional standard errors or pointwise normal intervals. Evaluation of \(\beta\) quantiles uses SciPy floating-point routines; these are statistical confidence statements, distinct from the previous directed-arithmetic Gaussian decision certificates.

\subsubsection{Finite-grid maximum risks}\label{finite-grid-maximum-risks}

For each n let \(R_{\rm rule}^{\rm grid}\) be the maximum of the five true expected calibration losses at the fixed t cells. If cellwise intervals are {[}L\_i,U\_i{]}, then

\[
\max_iL_i\le R_{\rm rule}^{\rm grid}\le\max_iU_i.
\]

For the improvement in grid maximum risk, subtract intervals: its lower bound is max\_i L\_baseline,i minus max\_i U\_optimized,i. The same simultaneous event covers all these derived comparisons. This is a five-cell statement, not a continuous-window maximum or finite-n minimax theorem.

Reference: Andreas Maurer and Massimiliano Pontil, \emph{Empirical Bernstein Bounds and Sample Variance Penalization}, COLT 2009, Theorem 4. https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/\textasciitilde colt2009/papers/012.pdf

\subsection{Unknown local visual environments: independent confirmation}\label{visual-confirmation}

\paragraph{Setup and information.} We next test prospective probability prediction on real visual scores, using the frozen ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 features and four prespecified CIFAR-10 class pairs. Official training features determine a fixed projection. For each pair, the official test empirical baseline determines one root $q_0$ and its constants; all environments retain $x_0=q_0$. With centered, unit-variance baseline entropy velocity $h_0(z)$, we draw images with weights proportional to $1+\theta h_0(z)$, where $\theta=t/(c\sqrt n)$ and $c=\sigma_0/(\lambda_0s_0)$. The generator knows $t$; predictors receive only independent unlabeled calibration scores and the declared baseline constants. They do not receive target roots, labels, generating coordinates or reference exits. Unlike the theoretical construction, reweighting labeled images changes the complete classification-risk function: risk is evaluated under each environment's actual weighted label law.

\paragraph{Exploration and independent confirmation.} The exploratory study covered four pairs, $n\in\{32,128,512\}$ and $t\in\{-1,-.5,0,.5,1\}$, with 1,024 reference paths per environment and 256 calibration repetitions per budget. Fixed relative calibration noise $r=c\sqrt{n/M}\in\{.5,1,2\}$ gave an error plateau across batch sizes; the sequence $r=2,1,.5$ gave decreasing error. These are finite-grid trends conditional on fixed image pools, not new independent images or a consistency theorem for a finite collection of frozen rules. All 36 fixed-ratio settings favored the optimized rule in maximum-MAE point estimates, but their simultaneous improvement intervals crossed zero. We therefore fixed a new confirmation design before generating its samples, rather than pooling exploration into confirmation.

\paragraph{Fixed design and scoring.} Confirmation retains all four pairs, fixes $n=128$, $K=r=1$, and uses the same five-point $t$ grid. Each of the 20 environments has 16,384 fresh reference paths and 4,096 independent calibration repetitions, totaling 327,680 paths and 81,920 repetitions. We use $\eta=.1$, slope $w=2$, tolerance $\epsilon=.02$, baseline prior $.5$, $M=\operatorname{round}(c^2n)$, and a fixed 4,000-update cap for every pair. Five predictors share each calibration repetition: constant $.5$, empirical-root plug-in, raw influence-function moment, window-clipped moment, and the previously frozen Gaussian step rule. Root failures would fall back to $.5$ and remain in the score. The primary comparison is clipped minus optimized five-point maximum expected MAE, separately for each pair.

\paragraph{Uncertainty and results.} We allocate error $.0125$ to each pair, giving at least 95\% simultaneous coverage across all four pairs, five rules and recorded paired comparisons by a union bound. Censor-compatible exact one-sided binomial reference bounds and conditional empirical-Bernstein calibration bounds account for both uncertainty layers. Maximum-risk differences are obtained by subtracting maximum-risk intervals, not by maximizing cellwise paired gains. The optimized rule has a lower grid maximum MAE in every pair. Its improvement over clipping has a positive interval in three pairs; automobile/truck remains unresolved, with interval $[-.0030,.1032]$. Comparisons with constant, empirical-root and raw-moment baselines have positive improvement intervals in all four pairs. These are finite-grid comparisons, with no claim of pointwise dominance.

\paragraph{Dynamics diagnostics and scope.} There are three censored reference paths and no empirical-root failures. The maximum point-estimate discrepancy between actual right-risk-exit probability and $\Phi(t)$ is $.01046$. Initial-safe-component exit and actual risk exit have different times on 18,378 paths, but no direction disagreement among paths with observed risk exits; censored paths remain in the bounds. The optimized rule trades endpoint accuracy for a smaller central peak. This confirms a reproducible prediction improvement on the declared finite visual grid. It does not validate shared risk on images, unknown-baseline robustness, corruption transfer, continuous-window finite-$n$ minimax optimality or exit-time accuracy.
