\begin{abstract}
Entropy adaptation can improve a classifier before making it worse than its frozen initialization. We study when this risk crossing occurs for actual mini-batch entropy updates of a scalar decision threshold under simultaneous feature and prior shift. Distributional geometry determines stability; near an unstable entropy peak, a nonlinear conjugacy gives a joint approximation for exit direction and integer crossing time. Fixed positive risk tolerance yields logarithmic batch-size delays under critical initialization, whereas inverse-batch tolerance gives finite-step autoregressive exit. Independent field calibration introduces a second scale: uncertainty in the entropy peak can dominate SGD noise even when the field is accurately estimated in absolute terms. Controlled frozen-feature experiments distinguish explanation from independent prediction. On a common visual subset, accurate target empirical geometry reduces mean exit-CDF error from .432 to .059, while source calibration fails. Independent unlabeled field estimation improves with budget but leaves a separate risk-boundary gap. Noise ablations identify continuing noise during slow far-field passage as an important source of finite-parameter tail error. The results characterize risk exit in a declared scalar model; they do not provide a completed unlabeled stopping algorithm or a guarantee for general neural-network adaptation.
\end{abstract}
