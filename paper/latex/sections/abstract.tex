\begin{abstract}
Predicting the consequences of entropy adaptation requires knowledge of target-domain dynamics, even when classification risk is known. We study this distinction for a scalar threshold following actual fresh-batch entropy-gradient updates. Target environments share initialization and their complete classification-risk function, while unlabeled marginals move an unstable entropy root on the batch-noise scale. A uniform exit-direction approximation connects this future trajectory to an independent calibration experiment. Around a known baseline, uniformly consistent prediction is possible exactly when calibration size M grows faster than future batch size n.~At positive finite \(M/n\), the minimax risk converges to a bounded-parameter Gaussian decision value, for which we compute lower and continuous-window upper certificates. The same value holds over unknown uniformly bounded local directions: the entropy-gradient tilt is already least favorable for root displacement relative to Fisher information. A frozen prediction rule improves five-cell maximum MAE over raw and clipped moment rules in real entropy-SGD confirmation, with simultaneous intervals accounting for calibration and reference-trajectory uncertainty. Independent confirmation on frozen CIFAR-10 features reduces five-point maximum MAE relative to clipping in all four class pairs by point estimate, with positive simultaneous improvement intervals in three; this visual experiment is conditional on known empirical baselines and has environment-specific risk. These results concern local scalar dynamics at fixed step size, not unknown baselines or general neural-network safety.
\end{abstract}
