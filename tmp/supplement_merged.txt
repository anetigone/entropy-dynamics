 309   8B.6. Contribution and open questions
 310   We study the independent information required to predict the direction of a future risk exit under entropy adaptation. We
 311   construct a scalar family with fixed initialization and an identical complete classification-risk function across environments,
 312   while the unknown unlabeled marginal moves an unstable entropy root on the mini-batch noise scale. A uniform approximation
 313   for the actual nonlinear SGD direction connects this dynamical sensitivity to a statistical estimation problem. Within this
 314   known family and at fixed positive step size, uniform consistency is possible exactly when the independent calibration size
 315   grows faster than the SGD batch size. When their ratio tends to zero, the exact limiting minimax risk is attained by the constant
 316   forecast one half. At positive finite ratios, an explicit moment predictor instead has a nonvanishing worst-case risk limit
 317   governed by an arctangent law; this predictor-specific limit is not a minimax lower bound.
 318      The finite-ratio problem is continued in §8B.7 below. Global sharp rates below the exit-approximation remainder, robustness
 319   to a misspecified tilt direction, broader unknown score families, joint vanishing-step limits and neural-network adaptation
 320   remain separate questions. Literature-priority assessment remains necessary.

 635   5. Contribution and boundary
 636   The Cauchy–Schwarz and tangent-space projection principles are classical semiparametric efficiency geometry. The added
 637   model-specific content is the identification psi=U0/(lambda s), its connection to a uniform true-SGD risk-exit functional, the
 638   shared-risk realization of the bounded perturbation class, and the minimax extension beyond a known direction.
 639      This result is local around a known Q0. If Q0, its root, lambda or s must be learned from a separate source sample, that
 640   sample’s accuracy and its relation to n enter a new information budget. Arbitrary unknown baselines, perturbations outside the
 641   nˆ{-1/2} neighborhood, growing B loc, online trajectory observation and eta->0 are not covered.
 642      Classical context: van der Laan and Gruber (2016), One-Step Targeted Minimum Loss-based Estimation Based on Universal
 643   Least Favorable One-Dimensional Submodels, discusses scores aligned with efficient influence curves. This manuscript does
 644   not claim that principle or TMLE methodology as a new contribution. https://doi.org/10.1515/ijb-2015-0054

 645   6. Finite baseline information and conditional prediction
 646   The actual baseline is now unknown within a fixed regular density neighborhood of a known reference. An independent
 647   baseline sample selects the initialization; the target conditions on that realized initialization. The complete common risk
 648   is retained. Results F.1–F.11 and their proofs follow. Their Gaussian checks do not constitute actual-SGD or BN/Tent

1039   8. What this extension does and does not establish

1040      8.1. A genuine unknown baseline, with a controlled reference class The unknown P ranges over a fixed infinite-
1041   dimensional density neighborhood; it is not merely a Gaussian variance parameter. Its root can differ from the known reference
1042   root by a fixed small amount. The forecast estimates its root-dependent slope and noise scale from BL .
1043      The reference neighborhood, common risk and root bracket remain known. Thus the result removes exact baseline
1044   knowledge within a declared regular class. It does not remove every oracle component of the original risk-isolation experiment.
1045      The Gaussian reference supplies a concrete legal construction. The proofs use uniform drift smoothness, a positive root
1046   derivative, nonzero root noise, a common safe-region geometry, and a density lower bound for finite exit. They do not assume
1047   the actual unknown baseline has Gaussian class conditionals.

1048      8.2. Conditional target versus averaging over initialization Equation (F.21) also implies, for each fixed u > 0,

1049                                           sup Pr{|πn (P, k; BL ) − Φ(Sn )| > u} −→ 0.
                                               P,k B

1050   It does not assert a bound for every possible realized baseline sample.
1051       At K = 0, the marginal target approaches 1/2, but the conditional target is approximately Φ(aP ZL ). The positive value
1052   in (F.35) measures uncertainty about this realized conditional probability when the baseline root is unknown. Averaging the
1053   initialization before defining the loss would remove precisely this difficulty.

1054       8.3. Budget interpretation For L ≍ n, the baseline error is of the same order as the stochastic seed of future SGD. This
1055   does not force L/n → ∞, since that error is part of the realized target and can be measured through target drift at the realized
1056   initialization.
1057       Finite L still changes the optimal decision rule and its minimax value. At K = 0, baseline and target observations estimate
1058   a common root, giving the explicit pooling rule. For K > 0, pooling without accounting for the bounded unknown root
1059   displacement can be biased; the posterior-median problem (F.33) describes the appropriate tradeoff.
1060       When L/n → 0, the compact-seed argument in this chapter no longer applies. The Gaussian model alone suggests that
1061   probabilities may saturate and prediction may become easier. For example, its raw upper bound tends to zero when a → ∞
1062   and b/a → 0. This is not an actual-SGD theorem for that regime, and Corollary F.10 must not be extrapolated to it.

1063      8.4. What still requires numerical or experimental work The general-K value is characterized by (F.29)/(F.33), with
1064   matching asymptotic lower and upper bounds. This chapter does not compute a least-favorable prior or produce new numerical
1065   continuous-window certificates for it. Proposition F.7 supplies a direct starting point for that task.
1066      The scale estimates are feasible and the raw and K = 0 optimal rules are explicit. No actual-SGD confirmation is claimed
1067   here. Such confirmation must resample the baseline sample as an outer level, hold each realized initialization fixed across
1068   predictor comparisons, and retain reference-trajectory uncertainty conditional on that initialization.
1069      The original optimized fixed-window rule is not automatically valid in this new experiment. Equation (F.40) is a structural
1070   warning against transferring its restricted output range.

       11. What would make a stopping rule observable?                                                                                       2090

       The risk boundaries used above depend on the target parameters and serve as analytical objects. They do not themselves define         2091
       an implementable unlabeled rule.                                                                                                      2092
          A generic certificate clarifies the additional requirement. Let ϑ = (δ, p), with known anchor parameters, and suppose              2093
       label-free, nested confidence sets Ct satisfy                                                                                         2094

                                                        Pr(ϑ ∈ Ct for all t) ≥ 1 − α.                                                        2095

       For a proposed threshold b′ , accept only if                                                                                          2096
                                                         sup {Rϑ (b′ ) − Rϑ (b0 )} ≤ ϵ.                                                      2097
                                                        ϑ∈Ct

       On the coverage event every accepted iterate is safe, including data-dependent proposals. This follows directly from the              2098
       supremum and is a generic baseline.                                                                                                   2099
          To become a useful algorithm, this construction needs a sequential confidence-set method under the stated observation              2100
       model, accounting for repeated inspection and proposal dependence. It also needs a nontrivial acceptance and improvement              2101
       guarantee. Ordinary fixed-time intervals repeatedly reused do not suffice. Anchor error, unknown component scales, orientation        2102
       mistakes, and model misspecification require explicit treatment.                                                                      2103
          Appropriate empirical comparators include frozen source, original entropy updates, logit-adjusted entropy updates, mean            2104
       recentering, an oracle risk threshold, and one-time unlabeled calibration with a frozen plug-in estimate. Known anchors               2105
       supply more information than strict weights-only source-free deployment. No universal claim about all unlabeled adaptation is         2106
       implied.                                                                                                                              2107

       12. Limitations and falsifiable scope                                                                                                 2108

       The integrated extension supplies proofs for independent-geometry prediction error and regular boundary perturbations under           2109
       explicit assumptions. Independent verification and alignment with visual events remain necessary. The expanded calibration            2110
       grid now covers 144 cells with 20 repetitions, including nonbalanced priors and nonzero offsets, but omits offset +2 and does         2111
       not exactly test target-centered critical limits. Noise ablations support continuing far-field noise as an important source of tail   2112
       dispersion in three single-side-dominated settings; a combined early-seed and passage-noise prediction for mixed directions           2113
       remains open. Unlabeled risk-boundary identification, the exact separation phase boundary, numerical approximation constants,         2114
       and unknown-anchor robustness remain unresolved.                                                                                      2115
          The completed frozen-encoder study retains official train/test splits and controls projected translation and priors. It supplies   2116
       useful conditional-oracle evidence, but the original independently calibrated prediction fails overall and the improved field-        2117
       calibration study still uses labeled oracle risk endpoints for its strongest results. Neither full BN adaptation nor scale/LA         2118
       comparisons have yet been validated by the available results. Image-space corruptions should not be claimed to satisfy a              2119
       Gaussian projection model without checking that approximation. The general scalar theorem now states such geometric                   2120
       conditions; their validity on each visual exit domain remains to be checked.                                                          2121
          The model omits nonstationarity, batch reuse, temporal dependence, learning-rate schedules, momentum, joint feature                2122
       learning, and multilayer normalization dynamics. A safe population basin is not permanent finite-batch safety. A high right-exit      2123
       probability is not a safety probability. A fixed-tolerance logarithmic horizon is not an improvement guarantee, and a longer          2124
       horizon under larger batch size is not universal at fixed prior.                                                                      2125
          The contribution would be insufficient if its statements followed immediately from accepted entropy-drift results plus a           2126
       generic unstable-exit theorem without a meaningful new risk prediction. The standard mechanisms are acknowledged explicitly.          2127
       The case for a contribution must instead rest on the actual recursion, risk-defined boundary, finite-parameter predictions, and       2128
       tolerance distinction, followed by a direct comparison with existing results.                                                         2129
